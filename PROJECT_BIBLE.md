# Chapter 1 - Product Vision

## Product Overview

Nhu Villas is a luxury villa booking platform for guests who want a calm, private, refined resort experience rather than a mass-market hotel booking flow.

The product must help guests:

- Discover villas through emotion, place, and atmosphere.
- Understand villa quality quickly and confidently.
- Check availability without friction.
- Complete booking with clear pricing, rules, and confirmation.
- Return to manage bookings, wishlist villas, and review their stays.

The product must help operators:

- Manage villas, media, pricing, bookings, customers, reviews, vouchers, and reports.
- Maintain operational trust through accurate availability and booking status.
- Grow direct bookings through SEO, luxury presentation, conversion-focused journeys, and reliable post-booking communication.

## Vision

Nhu Villas becomes the digital front door of a private villa resort brand: quiet, cinematic, trustworthy, and conversion-ready.

The site should feel less like a hotel inventory system and more like a curated invitation into a place.

## Mission

Create a premium direct-booking experience where guests feel:

- The villas are real and desirable.
- The stay is private and carefully hosted.
- The booking process is transparent.
- The brand is refined, calm, and trustworthy.
- Every interaction feels intentional.

## Core Value

Core product values:

- Privacy over noise.
- Clarity over persuasion pressure.
- Atmosphere over decoration.
- Trust over urgency tricks.
- Craft over generic templates.
- Direct booking confidence over marketplace confusion.

## Business Goal

Primary business goals:

- Increase qualified direct booking inquiries and confirmed bookings.
- Reduce dependency on third-party travel marketplaces.
- Raise perceived brand value and price confidence.
- Improve operational efficiency for booking, customer, review, and villa management.
- Build a scalable platform for future marketplace, membership, loyalty, and concierge services.

## Target Market

Primary market:

- Domestic and international luxury travelers.
- Couples, families, and small groups seeking private villa stays.
- Guests who value calm, design, service, nature, privacy, and trusted direct booking.

Secondary market:

- Corporate retreats and small executive gatherings.
- Wedding, anniversary, and honeymoon travelers.
- Long-stay guests seeking premium privacy.

## Success Metrics

Product metrics:

- Organic search visibility growth.
- Villa detail page engagement.
- Booking CTA click rate.
- Availability search completion rate.
- Booking form completion rate.
- Wishlist usage.
- Repeat guest account usage.
- Review submission rate.
- Admin task completion speed.

Quality metrics:

- SEO score >= 98.
- Performance score >= 95.
- Accessibility score >= 95.
- Responsive score >= 95.
- UI score >= 95.
- UX score >= 95.
- Luxury experience score >= 95.
- Motion score >= 95.
- Conversion score >= 95.

Operational metrics:

- Booking status accuracy.
- Payment deadline handling accuracy.
- Email delivery reliability.
- Admin report correctness.
- Error rate.
- API latency.
- Uptime.

## Product Positioning

Nhu Villas is positioned as a luxury direct-booking experience, not a commodity hotel listing site.

It should be:

- More intimate than Booking.com.
- More curated than Airbnb.
- More narrative than a standard resort website.
- More conversion-focused than a portfolio site.
- More operationally complete than a visual landing page.

It must never feel like:

- A SaaS dashboard.
- A Bootstrap hotel template.
- A Material UI booking app.
- A generic travel marketplace.
- A real-estate listing portal.

# Chapter 2 - Brand Guideline

## Brand Story

Nhu Villas represents private, intentional hospitality: villas shaped by quiet architecture, natural materials, soft light, garden rhythm, and careful hosting.

The brand story should speak through:

- Spatial calm.
- Local atmosphere.
- Tactile materials.
- Slow mornings and golden evenings.
- Private pools, gardens, terraces, and open-air living.
- Human service that feels present but never intrusive.

The story is not about luxury as excess. It is luxury as restraint, privacy, silence, and care.

## Brand Personality

Nhu Villas is:

- Calm.
- Warm.
- Private.
- Refined.
- Sensory.
- Trustworthy.
- Intimate.
- Editorial.
- Place-specific.

Nhu Villas is not:

- Loud.
- Trend-chasing.
- Corporate.
- Overly playful.
- Discount-led.
- Generic.
- Dashboard-like.
- Template-like.

## Tone of Voice

Voice principles:

- Speak with quiet confidence.
- Use concrete sensory language.
- Avoid inflated promises.
- Avoid generic travel cliches.
- Prefer short, elegant sentences.
- Make policies and prices plain.
- Make booking actions clear and direct.

Good tone examples:

- "A private pool villa shaped for slow mornings and open-air evenings."
- "Check availability for your preferred dates."
- "Your request has been received. We will confirm availability and payment details shortly."

Avoid:

- "Best deal ever."
- "Unforgettable paradise experience!!!"
- "Book now before it is too late."
- "Premium solution for hospitality management."

## Visual Identity

Visual identity must feel:

- Image-led.
- Quiet.
- Spacious.
- Natural.
- Editorial.
- Premium.
- Highly readable.

Preferred visual qualities:

- Warm natural light.
- Stone, wood, linen, greenery, still water.
- Large atmospheric photography.
- Editorial typography.
- Subtle borders and material surfaces.
- Soft contrast instead of harsh blocks.
- Layouts with breathing room.

Typography direction:

- Display type should feel elegant, resort-like, and editorial.
- Body type should be clean, readable, and modern.
- Headings should avoid excessive line wraps.
- Buttons and labels should be concise.

Color direction:

- Natural neutrals.
- Warm off-white.
- Deep green, charcoal, muted stone, soft gold accents.
- No loud neon.
- No pure template blue.
- No dashboard-gray dominance.

## Luxury Direction

Luxury is expressed through:

- Fewer elements, better placed.
- Real photography and honest space.
- Calm pacing.
- Clear hierarchy.
- Refined micro-interactions.
- High-quality responsive art direction.
- Trustworthy booking transparency.

Every public page should pass the "quiet luxury" test:

- Does it feel calm in the first 5 seconds?
- Does the image reveal the property or experience clearly?
- Is the copy restrained and useful?
- Is the booking action clear without feeling aggressive?
- Does the page feel more like a luxury resort than a software product?

## Inspiration

Primary references:

- Aman Resorts: silence, restraint, place-led luxury.
- Six Senses: wellness, nature, sensory calm.
- Rosewood Hotels: refined editorial hospitality.
- Airbnb Luxe: curated private stay discovery.

These references guide feeling and quality, not direct copying.

## Things That Must Never Be Used

Never use:

- Bootstrap visual style.
- Material UI visual style.
- SaaS landing page sections.
- Dashboard UI for public pages.
- Generic hotel template blocks.
- Loud gradients as primary identity.
- Decorative blobs or meaningless shapes.
- Fake urgency countdowns.
- Discount-heavy hero messaging.
- Cluttered card grids.
- Low-quality stock imagery.
- Tiny unreadable image thumbnails for core villa decisions.
- Animation that harms performance or accessibility.

# Chapter 3 - Target Customer

## Persona

Primary persona: The Private Luxury Traveler

- Age: 28-55.
- Travel style: private, curated, comfort-driven.
- Budget: mid-high to luxury.
- Device behavior: mobile discovery, desktop comparison, mobile booking follow-up.
- Decision drivers: imagery, trust, privacy, location, amenities, price clarity, reviews, service confidence.

Secondary persona: The Family Planner

- Needs multiple rooms, safety, convenience, kitchen/living space, pool, nearby activities.
- Compares details carefully.
- Needs clear rules, cancellation, guest capacity, and child/family suitability.

Secondary persona: The Occasion Booker

- Booking for honeymoon, anniversary, birthday, retreat, or private gathering.
- Needs emotional confidence and service reassurance.
- Responds strongly to photography, reviews, and concierge-style support.

Admin persona: The Resort Operator

- Needs fast access to bookings, room inventory, customers, reviews, pricing, vouchers, reports, and media.
- Values clarity, correctness, auditability, and low operational risk.

## Customer Segment

Segments:

- Couples seeking privacy.
- Families seeking premium shared space.
- Groups seeking villa stays.
- International travelers needing trust and clarity.
- Returning guests who need quick rebooking and profile access.

## Customer Goals

Guests want to:

- See the villa clearly.
- Understand what is included.
- Know whether dates are available.
- Understand final price.
- Trust the booking process.
- Receive confirmation and next steps.
- Save favorite villas.
- Read real reviews.
- Contact the property when needed.

Admins want to:

- Confirm and manage bookings.
- Avoid double booking.
- Update villa information.
- Manage media and amenities.
- Track customers and reviews.
- Export reports.
- Monitor operations.

## Pain Points

Guest pain points:

- Unclear availability.
- Hidden fees.
- Weak photography.
- Generic villa descriptions.
- Slow pages.
- Hard mobile navigation.
- Unclear cancellation/refund policy.
- No confirmation confidence.
- Booking forms that feel too long too early.

Admin pain points:

- Booking status confusion.
- Manual availability checking.
- Fragmented customer information.
- Review moderation friction.
- Voucher misuse.
- Report exports that do not match operations.

## Customer Journey

Discovery:

- Guest lands from search, social, referral, or direct URL.
- First need: understand brand quality and place.
- Primary page responsibility: create trust and desire quickly.

Consideration:

- Guest browses villa collection and detail pages.
- Needs to compare villas by capacity, price, amenities, and imagery.
- Reviews and policies matter.

Intent:

- Guest checks availability.
- Guest selects dates and guests.
- Guest reviews pricing and rules.

Booking:

- Guest submits booking or payment path.
- Guest receives clear confirmation and email.
- Booking status is trackable.

Post-booking:

- Guest can view booking history.
- Guest can cancel if policy allows.
- Guest receives notifications.

Post-stay:

- Guest can leave a review.
- Guest may wishlist or rebook.

## Booking Journey

Required booking journey:

1. Enter from hero CTA, villa card CTA, villa detail booking card, or navigation CTA.
2. Select dates.
3. Select guests.
4. Check availability.
5. Review villa, stay dates, guest count, price, taxes/fees if applicable, voucher, rules, and cancellation terms.
6. Submit booking request or payment step.
7. See success state with booking reference and next steps.
8. Receive confirmation email.
9. Admin reviews or system confirms according to business rules.
10. Guest can track booking status.

The journey must avoid surprises. Any unavailable date, invalid voucher, capacity issue, or payment requirement must be shown before final submission.

# Chapter 5 - Home Page Specification

## Hero

UX goal:

- Immediately communicate private luxury resort quality.
- Make the brand/place memorable within 5 seconds.
- Start the booking path without pressure.

Required content:

- One clear H1.
- Short supporting copy.
- Primary CTA: Check Availability.
- Secondary CTA: Explore Villas or View Villas.
- Real resort/villa imagery.

Design behavior:

- Image-led, not card-led.
- Text must be readable over media.
- CTA visible without scrolling on common laptop viewports.
- No busy badges or trust strips inside the hero.

SEO:

- Hero H1 must be real text.
- Metadata must align with hero promise.
- One H1 per page.

Responsive:

- Mobile crop must keep the villa or atmosphere visible.
- CTA must remain accessible.
- No text overlap.

Accessibility:

- Sufficient contrast.
- Keyboard-reachable CTAs.
- Meaningful image alt or decorative handling.

## Story

UX goal:

- Explain what makes Nhu Villas distinct.
- Convert visual desire into brand trust.

Content:

- Short editorial narrative.
- Place, privacy, design, service, nature.
- Avoid generic resort copy.

Motion:

- Gentle reveal.
- Scroll pacing should feel calm.

## Villa Collection

UX goal:

- Give guests a fast route to the right villa.
- Show enough information to compare without overwhelming.

Required:

- Villa cards with real image, name, capacity, key amenities, starting price or price signal, CTA.

- Card interactions must be elegant and stable.

- Primary action should move to detail or availability.

UX goal:

- Convert after emotional and informational confidence.

# Chapter 6 - Villa Collection

## Search

Search should help guests find suitable villas by:

- Date.
- Guest count.
- Keyword or villa name if useful.
- Location or area if multiple locations exist.

Search must not feel like an enterprise filter panel. It should be calm, concise, and easy to reset.

## Filter

Recommended filters:

- Guests.
- Bedrooms.
- Private pool.
- Price range.
- Amenities.
- Availability.
- Experience type.

Filter behavior:

- Mobile filters open in an accessible drawer or sheet.
- Applied filters are visible.
- Reset is available.
- Empty state is helpful.

## Sort

Sort options:

- Recommended.
- Price low to high.
- Price high to low.
- Capacity.
- Newest or featured if business supports it.

Default sort should be curated/recommended.

## Card

Villa card must show:

- Strong image.
- Villa name.
- Short descriptor.
- Capacity.
- Bedrooms if available.
- Key amenities.
- Price signal.
- Review/rating if available.
- CTA.
- Wishlist action if logged-in or available.

Card must not:

- Look like a SaaS card.
- Overuse shadows.
- Crop core image subjects badly.
- Hide key decision information.

## Loading

Loading state:

- Use elegant skeletons.
- Preserve layout dimensions.
- Avoid layout shift.
- Do not show spinner-only full pages when skeleton is possible.

## Empty State

Empty state must:

- Explain no villas match the current selection.
- Offer reset filters.
- Offer contact or alternate dates when useful.
- Preserve brand tone.

## Error State

Error state must:

- Explain the issue in human language.
- Offer retry.
- Offer contact if booking-critical.
- Avoid technical stack traces.

# Chapter 8 - Booking Experience

## Booking Flow

Booking must feel calm and certain.

Required steps:

1. Select villa.
2. Select dates.
3. Select guests.
4. Validate availability.
5. Review price and policies.
6. Enter guest details.
7. Apply voucher if available.
8. Submit booking or proceed to payment.
9. Receive success confirmation.
10. Receive email notification.

The flow must show progress when multi-step.

## Calendar

Calendar must:

- Show unavailable dates.
- Prevent impossible selection.
- Support keyboard use.
- Support mobile selection.
- Explain minimum stay rules if they exist.
- Avoid visual ambiguity between selected, unavailable, today, and hover states.

## Availability

Availability must be server-validated before booking submission.

The UI must handle:

- Dates became unavailable.
- Guest count exceeds capacity.
- Villa inactive.
- Booking window closed.
- Network error.

## Guest Selector

Guest selector must:

- Distinguish adults, children, infants if business supports it.
- Respect capacity.
- Explain capacity limits.
- Be easy on mobile.

## Pricing

Pricing must be transparent.

Required:

- Nightly price.
- Number of nights.
- Fees/taxes if applicable.
- Voucher discount.
- Total.
- Payment deadline if applicable.
- Deposit/full-payment rule if applicable.

Never hide fees until the final step.

## Confirmation

Before final submission, show:

- Villa.
- Dates.
- Guests.
- Guest contact.
- Total price.
- Voucher.
- Rules accepted.
- Cancellation/refund summary.

## Success

Success state must show:

- Booking reference.
- Status.
- Next steps.
- Email confirmation note.
- Link to booking history.
- Contact option.

## Email

Email communication must include:

- Booking received.
- Booking approved.
- Booking rejected.
- Payment needed.
- Payment received.
- Cancellation.
- Password reset.
- Verification.

Email tone must match brand: clear, calm, and trustworthy.

- Avoid becoming a link dump.

## Modal

- Trap focus.
- Close with Escape.
- Restore focus after close.
- Prevent background scroll when needed.
- Have clear title and action area.

## Calendar

Calendar must:

- Show state clearly.
- Be keyboard accessible.
- Work on mobile.
- Respect unavailable dates.
- Avoid color-only meaning.

## Form

Forms must:

- Have labels.
- Show validation near fields.
- Preserve user input when errors happen.
- Avoid unnecessary fields.
- Support loading and success states.

## Toast

Toasts must:

- Be brief.
- Be non-blocking unless critical.
- Not hide form validation.
- Be accessible through status/live region behavior.

## Skeleton

Skeletons must:

- Match final layout dimensions.
- Avoid excessive shimmer.
- Prevent layout shift.

## Empty State

Empty states must:

- Explain what happened.
- Offer next action.
- Match brand tone.
- Avoid blame.

## Error State

Error states must:

- Explain the problem.

- Hide technical implementation details.

# Chapter 12 - Motion System

## Hero Timeline

Hero motion should:

- Start with media settling softly.
- Reveal headline after visual context.
- Reveal supporting copy after headline.
- Reveal CTA last.
- Use opacity and transform primarily.
- Stop or simplify when reduced motion is requested.

Motion must never delay access to the CTA.

## Scroll Storytelling

Scroll storytelling should:

- Create calm progression.
- Support narrative sections.
- Avoid excessive pinning.
- Preserve reading comfort.
- Never trap users.

## GSAP

GSAP should be used for:

- Hero timeline.
- Section reveals.
- Gallery transitions.
- Scroll-driven storytelling where valuable.

GSAP must not be used for trivial effects that CSS can handle better.

## Lenis

Lenis may be used for smooth scrolling when:

- It does not harm accessibility.
- It respects reduced motion.
- It does not break anchors.
- It does not create input lag.

## Reveal

Reveal motion should:

- Be subtle.
- Be consistent.
- Use restrained distance.
- Avoid every element animating independently.

## Gallery Motion

Gallery motion should:

- Feel tactile and premium.
- Preserve image inspection.
- Avoid motion blur that hides villa detail.
- Work with touch gestures.

## Micro Interaction

Micro-interactions should apply to:

- Buttons.
- Cards.
- Wishlist.
- Calendar dates.
- Form feedback.
- Mobile menu.

They must be small, fast, and meaningful.

# Chapter 13 - Business Rules

## Booking

Booking rules:

- A booking must reference one villa, one guest/user, date range, guest count, status, and price summary.
- Availability must be validated server-side.
- Double booking must be prevented.
- Booking status must be explicit.
- Guests must see next steps after submission.

Typical booking statuses:

- Pending.
- Approved.
- Rejected.
- Payment needed.
- Paid.
- Cancelled.
- Completed.

## Check-in

Check-in rules must define:

- Standard check-in time.
- Early check-in availability if applicable.
- Identification or contact requirements.
- Arrival instructions.

## Check-out

Check-out rules must define:

- Standard check-out time.
- Late check-out availability if applicable.
- Additional fee rule if applicable.

## Cancellation

Cancellation rules must define:

- Who can cancel.
- Until when cancellation is allowed.
- Refund implication.
- Notification behavior.
- Admin override policy if allowed.

## Refund

Refund rules must define:

- Refund eligibility.
- Refund amount.
- Payment method considerations.
- Processing time.
- Admin responsibility.

## Voucher

Voucher rules:

- Code must be validated server-side.
- Expired vouchers are invalid.

- Usage limits must be enforced if present.

Review rules:

- Only eligible guests should review when business requires verified stay.

## Notification

Notifications must support:

- Booking updates.
- Payment reminders.
- Admin alerts.
- Review prompts.
- Password/security communication.

Critical notifications should not rely only on in-app display; email is required for booking-critical events.

# Chapter 14 - Backend Specification

## Architecture

Target backend role:

- Spring Boot is the REST API layer.

Backend must separate:

- Controller/API boundary.
- DTO/request/response objects.
- Service/business logic.
- Repository/data access.
- Validation.
- Exception handling.
- Security.
- Logging and monitoring.

## API

API principles:

- Versioned under `/api/v1`.
- Resource-oriented.
- Predictable naming.
- Frontend-friendly.
- No leaking entity internals when DTOs are needed.
- Consistent success and error shapes.

API families:

- Villas/rooms.
- Amenities.
- Room types.
- Availability.
- Booking.
- Voucher.
- Review.
- Wishlist.
- Auth/account.
- Profile.

- SEO >= 98.
- Performance >= 95.
- Accessibility >= 95.

- Files changed.
